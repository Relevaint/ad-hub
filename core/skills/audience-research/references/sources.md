# Sources: where buyers speak, by business type

Load before the walk. Everything here is a default. The hard rules are in the skill:
quote only what was read, name every source walked or blocked, never invent a line.

## The reputation walk

Do what a buyer does before buying, in order, and write down the objections and
friction that appear (Observed, creative-strategy review):

1. Search the business by name, then the category plus the place or the problem. Read
   the first page: what a buyer sees first, what the business is known for, anything
   that looks like a warning.
2. The business itself: founders, best sellers and offers, the page the ad would send
   people to.
3. Press and listings, if any.
4. Where buyers compare: marketplaces, directories, review sites.
5. Where buyers ask: forums, question threads, community groups.
6. Video reviews and the comments under them. Comments are usually richer than the
   video. Many video pages do not expose comments to a reader; say so when they do not.
7. The business's own social accounts and the comments on its posts and ads.

## Review mining: the questions to ask of the material

For every batch of lines (Observed, creative-strategy review): what is praised, what is
complained about, what phrases stop you, what is trending up or down over time, and
what triggered the purchase. Add: what they used or considered before, and what they
say the offer is not for.

Reviews mean wherever this business's customers speak. For a service that is a call
log, a review page on a map, a neighbourhood group, a survey, or the owner's memory of
what customers ask. Record the owner's memory as Owner-confirmed, in their words, and
mark it as the owner's account rather than a customer's.

## By business type

**Online product, direct sale.** The deepest public record. Own-site reviews and the
export behind them; marketplace reviews of the product and of the nearest alternatives;
video reviews and their comments; forums for the hobby or problem; comments under the
business's own ads. Sort reviews by most recent as well as most liked; the trend
matters.

**Local service.** The map and directory review pages for the business and for the
competitors a buyer would call next; the business's own enquiry form, emails, call notes
and job notes (held by the business); neighbourhood and town groups where people ask
"anyone know a good..."; the owner's own account of what people ask on the phone. The
service area is part of who buys: lines from outside it count less.

**Professional service, consultancy, agency.** Client conversations and discovery call
notes (held by the business); the places buyers ask peers (industry forums, professional
networks, podcast and newsletter comments); review platforms for that kind of service;
testimonials the business has permission to use. Buyers here describe the trigger
(what broke, who left, what deadline) more than the service; capture the trigger.

**Regulated category (housing, credit, employment, health).** Same sources, with two
limits. Do not record lines that describe people by protected attributes as if they
were targeting instructions; a customer type is a situation, not a protected class. And
point at the business's compliance file in the audience file; do not restate its rules.

**New business, no customers yet.** Category research: reviews and threads about buyers
of the same kind of thing, in the same kind of place, and reviews of the nearest
alternatives (the shop, the do-it-yourself route, doing nothing). Label the whole file
category research. These are leads and assumptions until this business has its own
customers; the first real enquiries and jobs replace them. Tell the business to keep
every early customer conversation; that is the next run's best source.

## What counts as reading

- A page opened and read, with its link recorded. A line quoted from it is exact,
  including spelling.
- A file in the business repo, with its path recorded.
- A search result is a pointer. Its snippet is not a quote until the page is opened. If
  the page cannot be opened, record the source as not reached and leave the snippet out.
- A video is read only if its transcript or captions were read. Say so, or say it was
  not seen (claude-workflows review).

## Platform limits

Some sources block automated reading or sit behind a login. From any platform: try,
and when a source cannot be opened, record it by name in the Sources walked table with
the reason seen (blocked, login, not found) and move on. Never fill the gap from
memory. Which sources a given platform can reach changes over time; the operator's
handoff notes hold the dated observations, not this file.

## Privacy

No reviewer names, handles, photos or anything that identifies a private person. A
public review is quoted and linked; the link is the attribution. Customer emails and
call notes stay in the business's own files; `voc.md` holds the line, marked as held by
the business, and nothing else about the person. Raw exports are not copied into
`ads/`.
