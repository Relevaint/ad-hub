# Copy and hooks: the spec, the structures and the rubric

Load for every batch and every quick request. Defaults from the reviews; the hard rules
are in the skill. Named tactics are plays in `../../ad-strategy/references/plays.md`.

## The hook

Three things at once (Observed, hooks review): what the viewer reads (the first
on-screen text or first line of copy), what they hear (the first spoken words or sound),
and what is in the first frame (or the image, for a static). Write all three on every
ad. Judge them together: a strong line over a dull frame is a weak hook.

Test three to six hooks on one body, with big differences between them, not one word
changed (Observed, hooks review). The hook plays in the library are the starting menu;
a hook built from a real customer line often beats them.

## Structures, by style

- **Creator or customer on camera, structured.** Hook, the pain in their words, a
  relatable scene, the benefit, one next step. The person resembles the buyer and the
  story is true (course review, 12.1; creator-ads review).
- **Creator, simple.** Unscripted footage to music, captions carry the message.
- **Founder.** The problem met, the thing built, the objection answered before it is
  raised; the simple version is a plain photo, plain copy, one call to action (12.2).
- **Demonstration.** The product in use or the service done, to its result; one
  specific relatable use, or polished for premium (12.3).
- **Testimonial.** An honest review that admits a trade-off; a montage of varied real
  customers; always released (12.4).
- **Static.** Headline first, then the product or key visual, then support; the
  one-second test (static-ads review). Menu: infographic, headliner, benefit callouts,
  us against the alternative, transformation, grid, text only, lo-fi creator photo,
  golden nugget from a real review.
- **Carousel.** Cards that tell one sequence or show one catalogue; each card stands
  alone on the first glance.

## Copy principles

Defaults, from the static-ads, hooks and course reviews:

- Specific beats broad: a named situation, a real number, an exact outcome.
- Call out the situation in the first line, never the person's attributes.
- Clear beats clever: no puns, no wordplay, understood at a glance.
- The desire is the headline; the product is the means.
- Open a loop the landing page closes; the page must close it.
- Raise the doubt yourself when the market is sceptical.
- Say who it is not for, when the offer is narrow.
- Position against the thing people use by default, not against a named competitor.
- Reframe a price against a familiar spend only with the real price.
- One small, friendly next step for calls and consultations; a specific action for
  everything else, never a vague "learn more".
- A striking phrase from a real, cleared review beats anything written here.

Length is a placement fact: the platform layer holds the fields and their visible
limits. Write to them.

## Using the buyers' words

Lines from `research/voc.md` are the richest material there is. Quote them exactly and
cite the ID in the brief. A public, cleared review may appear as a testimonial with its
source. A private line (an email, a call note) shapes the language and never appears as
a quote in the ad until the business clears it in the proof table.

## Marked blanks

When a fact would make the ad stronger and is not on file, write the blank into the
draft where the fact would go: `[confirm: years serving the county]`. List every blank
in the file and in the reply. A blank is a question to the business, not a placeholder
to fill from what businesses like this usually say.

## The rubric

Score each ad on five lines, 1 to 5, with these anchors. Revise the weakest, rescore,
three passes at most, then present the best with its history.

| Line | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glance | A stranger cannot say what is sold | Says what, not for whom | Says what and for whom in one second |
| One job | Two goals or two audiences | One goal, audience implied | One goal, one customer type, one awareness level, matches the row |
| Hook | Generic opener; frame unrelated | Clear line; frame supports | Text, sound and first frame pull in the named type |
| Proof | A claim with no row behind it | Claims true but unspecific | Every claim specific and mapped; blanks marked |
| Finish | A brief, no asset | Asset exists, not inspected or wrong shape | Every shape made, inspected, text in safe zones |

An ad below 3 on Proof does not leave this skill. An ad below 3 on Finish stays
`copy-ready`.

## Copy standard

Customer-facing copy: no em dashes, no emojis, plain language, no filler, no hedging.
Required legal and brand strings exactly as the business wrote them. The hub carries no
voice; the business's own files do.
