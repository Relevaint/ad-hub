# Composition review: hierarchy, not mandatory minimalism

Use inside creative Pass 4, the separate Pass 6 critique and Pass 7 render QA.
The goal is a readable, distinctive ad, not the fewest objects. A rich environment,
ensemble, demonstration or information-dense format can serve the audience better.
Clean and rich treatments are both available; neither earns a pass by style alone.
Casting and rendering remain independent decisions.

## Make and challenge the hierarchy

1. **Name the intended read.** What should the viewer notice first, what supporting
   detail makes the situation meaningful, and where do product/benefit and action
   become clear? On video, answer for the opening and the turn/payoff, not every
   element at once. On static, the complete proposition must work at a glance.
2. **Find the competitors.** Look for props, faces, text blocks, bright colors,
   background activity, framing or motion that demand attention without helping that
   read. Distinguish useful narrative detail from decoration. A count of objects or
   a quota for whitespace cannot answer this.
3. **Recompose, don't flatten.** Remove, reduce contrast/scale, group, reposition or
   separate details in time. Preserve the unusual relationship, gesture, contrast or
   demonstration that makes this premise distinctive. Keep product clarity, truthful
   proof, required disclosures and the action. Do not shrink legal copy to pass.
4. **Compare the result.** Does the focal idea become easier to understand? Did a
   necessary audience cue, sense of scale, emotional context or product connection
   disappear? Restore or reorganize useful detail if the cleaner version is weaker.
   Record one compact verdict with a specific change, or why richness is necessary.

**Reject / rework:** If competing details obscure the intended read, return to
Pass 4 and recheck the connected whole in Pass 6. If simplification destroys the
premise, restore it or return to Pass 2/3. Do not pass a generic isolated object just
because its background is empty. A failed hierarchy gate cannot be averaged away.

In concepts-only work, describe the composition and its risks; do not generate a
comparison asset merely to run this review. In authorized production, inspect the
actual render at intended mobile/placement size, first without copy for the applicable
visual-alone gate, then with copy for reading order, product clarity and CTA. Look
at the actual pixels, not only a prompt or filename. If you cannot view an asset,
state that limit and do not claim visual QA. Apply this to video movement, overlays
and transitions as well as stills; it does not require static-looking videos.

## Learn from paired images without copying them

Open the repository-native images in the [comparison gallery](composition-examples/README.md).
Inspect the pixels when image viewing is available; text descriptions are not evidence
that you inspected them. The comparisons preserve original and simplified executions,
show what changed and what each gives up. They teach selective emphasis, not a
required cast, holiday, mug, empty backdrop, headline, brand voice or composition.

These are generated teaching examples, not real customer stories or approved current
product claims. Both versions can be useful. Copy, CTA treatment and framing also
change, so these are not controlled single-variable tests. Editorial preference and
visual legibility are not conversion evidence; only an appropriately designed audience
test can establish performance. Read current offer facts from the business repo.
