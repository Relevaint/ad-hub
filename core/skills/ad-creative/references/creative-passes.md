# Creative passes: build, challenge, then produce

Load for every concept, Batch, Quick and Remix request, before drafting. This is the
required creative workflow, not another menu of optional tips. It is self-contained:
no earlier conversation, particular campaign or example is needed. Keep the pass
outputs short; their purpose is to expose decisions and failures, not produce essays.

## Scope first

Read the requested deliverables literally. Concepts are not assets. A request for
concepts only stops after Pass 6: no image, audio or video generation, production-tool
calls, finished-asset claim or ledger promotion. If the user requests concepts plus
one image, produce that image only after its concept passes; leave the video a concept.

Batch and Quick use every pass. Remix preserves the proven element and runs the passes
on the proposed change and its connection to the whole ad; do not silently change the
experiment. Check mode diagnoses against the passes without rewriting unless asked.
A fixed brief, cast, style or test variable stays fixed. If it prevents a viable ad,
name the conflict instead of changing strategy or scope without authority.

## Pass 1. Fundamentals, before creative

Output a compact brief: audience and recognizable situation; awareness and surface;
one desire/problem; offer and defensible benefit; proof available and claims forbidden;
one message and action; brand/category constraints; requested formats and output scope.
Separate supplied facts from assumptions. Missing research does not block ideation,
but an unknown fact cannot become a claim. Use the skill's claims and policy checks.

**Gate:** Can this ad do one clear job for this audience within the brief? If not,
resolve the brief or ask only for the blocking choice. Do not invent a customer story.

## Pass 2. Premises, not polished headlines

Develop materially different situations, contrasts, mechanisms or surprises from the
brief and fresh material in [opening-dynamics.md](opening-dynamics.md). Each candidate
gets: a one-sentence premise, why this audience would care, and why the product belongs
in its resolution. Vary the underlying idea, not just a phrase, character or color.
Use evidence honestly: a trend not actually observed is an idea, not trend research.

**Gate:** Reject interchangeable category slogans and ideas whose only strength is
nice wording. Reject novelty with no product-connected payoff. Preserve promising
candidates for treatment exploration; do not polish the first plausible one by habit.

## Pass 3. Cast and treatment are independent decisions

For the strongest premises, compare meaningfully different treatments before choosing.
Make two separate decisions:

- **Cast / agent of the action:** humans, animals, aliens, mythical beings, seasonal
  figures such as Santa or Cupid, objects, an environment, or no character. These are
  examples, not an exhaustive list or a required quota.
- **Rendering / visual treatment:** photoreal photography, cinematic 3D, cartoon,
  illustration, stop-motion, collage, practical footage, or another suitable language.

Nonhuman does not mean "turn the product or nearby prop into a talking character."
An animal or alien can look photoreal; a human can be a cartoon. Do not correlate cast
with rendering, default every idea to realistic people, or force fantasy into every
ad. Casting may change the entire premise, not merely replace the actor in one scene.

Output the chosen cast, treatment and a short reason; note the materially different
alternative rejected and why. Choose for audience recognition, clarity, freshness,
brand fit and feasible execution. Absurdity is available, not mandatory. A serious or
restricted-category brief may call for restraint. A supplied brand character or style
is a constraint, not permission to ignore the brief. Fictional scenes can illustrate;
never portray fabricated people or creatures as real customers or as product proof.

**Gate:** If the choice is only a default, return to treatment exploration. If the cast
changes the idea's logic, return to Pass 2 and recheck the product connection.

## Pass 4. Build the visual stop

Describe the actual first frame (video) or single image (static), not "scroll-stopping
visuals." Name the focal point, action/contrast, what is understood immediately, and
the question or interest it creates. Explain the audience relevance visible early.

For cold visual interruption surfaces, run a **visual-alone stop test**: mute the
sound and temporarily hide the headline/caption. Would a distracted stranger have a
specific reason to pause, without already wanting the product? Name that reason.
Beautiful, relatable, premium and emotional are not reasons by themselves. A headline
cannot rescue a generic frame. Restore copy and sound afterward to strengthen the idea.

This is a concept-stage prediction, not a measured thumb-stop rate. It does not require
shock, comedy or an impossible scene: an arresting demonstration or exact situation
can qualify. Search text ads, deliberate text-only work and proof/offer-led warm ads
use their selected opening dynamic instead; record why the visual-alone test is not
applicable. Do not quietly switch a cold visual request into an exception to avoid it.

**Gate:** A weak visual returns to Pass 2 or 3, not to headline polishing. Clutter that
needs explanation is a failure. An arbitrary spectacle unrelated to the offer fails.

## Pass 5. Connect opening, turn, payoff and action

Now write the hook as image, text and sound (sound: not applicable for a static), then
build the body and copy. On video: opening -> development -> turn -> product payoff ->
action. State when the product enters and which question it answers. The turn earns
its reveal; never attach a product screen to an unrelated joke. The scene establishes
relevance early even if product identification waits for the turn.

On a static, there is no timed reveal: visual, headline and support must form one
understandable proposition at a glance. State what the full image communicates and
how the product resolves its tension. Leave enough visual hierarchy for mobile size.

Output the sequence or composition, the key copy and CTA, plus factual claims mapped
to proof. Keep demonstration details clearly illustrative unless supported as real.

**Gate:** Product clarity is judged at the reveal/first complete proposition, not
necessarily the first frame. A demonstration or most-aware offer can identify it
immediately. If the payoff does not fulfill the opening's promise, return to Pass 2
or 4. If viewers could remember only the gag and not the benefit, revise the connection.

## Pass 6. Adversarial concept review, then revision

Review as a distracted viewer, not as the author defending the work. When available,
use a fresh reviewer given the brief, constraints and candidates, without the author's
sales pitch or self-scores. Otherwise perform a separate critique pass and say it was
self-review when reporting verification. Do not claim an independent review occurred.

For each concept, record a concrete reason for each verdict:

1. **Attention:** Does the opening earn a pause under the applicable Pass 4 test?
2. **Relevance / one job:** Is the situation for the intended audience, without
   relying on forbidden personal-attribute assertions or changing the brief?
3. **Promise / clarity:** Does the product payoff answer the opening and make the
   benefit and next action clear? On statics, does the complete image work at a glance?
4. **Proof / policy:** Are claims supportable and required rules preserved?
5. **Distinctness / execution:** Is this a distinct mechanism where the brief asks for
   variety, and can the idea be read quickly and actually produced? A deliberately
   matched pair or controlled variant is valid when that is the brief, not evidence
   of several independent ideas.

Score with [copy-and-hooks.md](copy-and-hooks.md), but **do not average away a failed
gate**. Hook and Glance must each reach 4/5, One job and Proof at least 3/5, and every
applicable pass gate must pass before a concept is recommended. Strong copy does not
compensate for a weak visual. Polish is not a substitute for a new premise.

On failure, state what failed, return to the named pass, change that part and review
the connected whole again. Use up to three revision cycles within the task; the limit
is a stopping boundary, not permission to pass weak work. If still failing, withhold
it from the recommended set and report the shortfall or exact blocker. Never pad a
requested count with failed concepts or quietly lower the bar.

Present surviving concepts, their opening/turn/payoff and a short reason they survived.
Keep the working notes compact and out of the user's way unless requested. A concept
that passes is **untested and concept-ready**, not a finished asset or proven winner.
Concepts-only work ends here. See the concept record in the creative schema if saved.

## Pass 7. Authorized production and execution review

Only produce the requested assets after Pass 6 passes. Inspect the actual image/video
at delivery size, not just the prompt: first frame, timing, readable text, safe zones,
cast/treatment consistency, claims and product demonstration. Watch a video to verify
its pacing; an unplayed file is unverified. Re-run the gates on the execution. Repair
a render failure or return to the concept if the idea cannot survive production.

Actual audience performance is established only by testing. Concept scores, a fresh
reviewer, a polished render and this workflow do not establish that an ad will win.
