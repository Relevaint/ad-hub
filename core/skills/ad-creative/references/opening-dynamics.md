## The principle

No one engine fits every video. The product enters when it becomes the answer
to a question the viewer now has. That timing is the whole framework. In a
demonstration it is second one. In a problem-and-consequence film it is after
the "oh no." In a story it is at the turn. For a most-aware viewer it is the
offer, plainly.

What decides the timing is the viewer's awareness on arrival, plus the surface.
A cold feed audience is usually unaware or problem-aware. Opening on the
product for them is opening on something they have no reason to care about
yet. A search audience typed a query, so they arrive with the question
already formed, and clarity beats curiosity.

## The two attentions, in order, at every level

1. Reflexive. Movement, a face, high contrast, a sudden sound, something that
   does not belong. Bought in a fraction of a second, and cheap. It buys the
   next moment, not the next ten.
2. Relevance. "This is about me, my problem, my kind of thing." This turns the
   reflex into a decision to keep watching. Earned by specificity: the right
   situation, the right words, the right object, shown rather than announced.

Reflex without relevance is the most common expensive mistake: a striking
opening the viewer leaves at second two because nothing told them it was for
them. Relevance without reflex is the second most common: the right message,
delivered so quietly the thumb never paused.

Relevance must land by second two, through the object, the setting, the
words, or the person. On a static, hook, turn, and close are simultaneous, so
the whole image carries relevance at once. On video, the turn lands later,
so the scene itself must signal who it is for early even while the product
waits.

## The table: awareness decides the opening

| The viewer is | Surface | Open on | Product enters | Mechanisms that fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware of the problem | Cold interruption (Meta feed, Reels) | Their situation, identity, or feeling. Not the problem yet, and not the product. | At the turn, as the different thing. | Violation of expectation (pattern-break), open question, spectacle or absurd messenger, recognizable pain as situation mirror. |
| Problem-aware | Cold or warm interruption | The pain, named the way they say it. | After the "oh no," as the fix. | Recognizable pain, threat to something they value, direct address, specificity. |
| Solution-aware | Search, warm feed, consideration | The contrast: why the usual fix fails, or the mechanism that is different. | Early, framed as the contrast. | Contrast (us against the alternative), result first, specificity, strong opinion. |
| Product-aware | Search, site visitors, warm retargeting | Proof, or a specific result. | Early. Brand and product early are right here. | Proof (real reviews and counts only, and only if true), result first, demonstration as spectacle. |
| Most-aware | Brand search, hot retargeting, email | The offer, plainly. | First. | Offer clarity, price or bundle or deadline, only if true and dated. |

Exception, any level: when the product in use is itself the spectacle or
the result (a demonstration that is visibly satisfying), the product is
the hook, at second one.

## The mechanism catalog

Each mechanism is a way to stop and hold, not a template. Pick one per ad,
not three phrasings of one. From the RTV creative-brain attention reference.

- Threat to something the viewer values (their car, money, standing, time).
  Works when the thing is theirs and the threat is plausible.
- Recognizable pain: a mirror of a moment they have lived. Works by
  recognition, not description. The moment must be specific enough to be
  theirs.
- Open question: the felt gap between what they know and what they want to
  know. Works only when the answer matters to them. A gap about something
  irrelevant is noise.
- Violation of expectation: something wrong, out of place, or the opposite
  of the category's usual opening. Works once per pattern. When everyone
  does it, it is the pattern.
- Result first: the after, with no context. Works when the result is visibly
  desirable and a little hard to believe, which creates the question "how."
- Specificity: an oddly exact detail signals a real story and rewards
  attention.
- Strong opinion, stated flatly. Works by tribal reflex: agree and you lean
  in, disagree and you lean in.
- Spectacle: scale, beauty, danger, precision. Works alone for a second or
  two, then needs relevance like everything else.
- Someone like me, or a moment from a shared culture. Works by identity.
  Fails when the "like me" is guessed wrong.
- Direct address: the viewer placed inside a situation. Works by simulation.

## The outside-the-box input (dynamic, refreshed every batch)

The table above selects the right approach. It does not generate a novel
idea. Selection is not generation. A batch built from the table alone will
be on-strategy and generic: the right shape with nothing alive in it. The
framework needs a second input, and that input is dynamic. It changes with
what is happening right now, and it must be refreshed before every batch,
because trends expire and patterns wear out. Violation of expectation
works once per pattern. When everyone does it, it is the pattern.

Three fresh inputs, pulled before every batch:

- Current trends, two layers. Ad Library adjacent categories first
  (flower, card, gift, plushie brands, Etsy sellers): record the
  mechanic, never the copy. But the Library only shows paid ads, so it
  misses organic internet trends, and by the time a trend is in the
  Library it has already been commercialized. The internet layer: social
  search on the niche, Threads trends, what top creators in adjacent
  niches are posting this week, TikTok Creative Center trending hashtags
  and songs, YouTube trending, Google Trends rising queries for the
  niche. There is no single firehose for "what is trending." Feeds are
  personalized, so the trend that matters is the one this audience sees,
  not the global one. Trend roundups and meme databases only name and
  date something already spotted, never discover it. The question per
  find is one line: the mechanic, and why it stops, plus the date seen.
  The viral layer (a sound, a format, a meme before it has a name) stays
  manual for now: no tool here watches video feeds at scale or catches a
  trend before it is named. Manual means capture, not invention: when
  something stops your own scroll, save the link, the date seen, and the
  mechanic. It is not sitting down to brainstorm trends on demand.
- Calendar. Holidays and relevant periods the audience is already
  feeling: Christmas, Valentine's, Mother's Day, Father's Day, graduation,
  and the business's own seasons. Plus current conditions everyone is
  living through right now: prices, seasons, big events. The calendar is
  forced material, not decoration. What is the audience already thinking
  about that the ad can collide with?
- Deliberate absurdity. An absurd messenger or one impossible rule, played
  completely straight: cute animals giving gifts, aliens singing to each
  other. Absurdity is an input, not an accident. It works when the absurd
  element proves a product truth (you do not need to sing to give a song),
  and fails when it is cute with no turn.

The collision rule. The idea is the row's mechanism crossed with fresh
material. Violation of expectation crossed with a current format. An open
question crossed with a holiday the audience is already feeling. Without
the collision you get one of two failures: on-strategy but generic (table
only), or novel but off-strategy (trend only). The usual filters still
apply: could any gift brand run it (if yes, it fails), does it assume the
viewer is already looking for the product (if yes, rewrite), claims gate,
policy check.

## The stop test and the promise

The ad stops the scroll in one second, sound off, from the image, the
headline, or both together. If neither does anything, kill it. What fails is
generic plus generic.

The opening is a promise, and everything after it is the promise being kept.
A hook the body does not fulfill is not a hook. It is a lie the viewer
discovers at second five, and they leave angrier than if the opening had
been dull. The turn must close the loop the hook opened. A loop opened but
never closed is a trick, not curiosity, and tricks do not convert.

## Worked example: the Your Song row (unaware, cold Meta, unknown category)

The song-gift category does not exist in buyers' heads yet, unlike cards
and flowers, so the ad's first job is to break a familiar gift pattern and
introduce something different. The hook never assumes the category.

- Hook: recognition plus tension. A familiar gift, broken: dead flowers,
  a burning card, a drawer of forgotten gifts. Or an open question with an
  ambiguous referent: "Did they all die?" The gap lives in the pronoun or
  the missing why. A closed statement answers itself and fails.
- Turn: only here does the song appear, as the different thing, and the
  turn names who it is for. Specific (your wife, your husband, your mom)
  or generic (your person, that special someone). Generic lets the viewer
  fill in their own person, which makes specific versus generic a testable
  variant.
- Close: one short line to the site. No price on the ad (current call,
  testable later as its own variable).
- The same business uses a different row on search: solution-aware and
  product-aware viewers typed a query, so product-led headlines are right
  there. One business, two rows, decided by awareness and surface.

## How to use it in a batch

1. Read the row from the ledger row's awareness tag plus the surface.
1b. Pull fresh material before choosing: current trends, the calendar,
    and deliberate absurdity. This pull is repeated every batch.
2. Pick a mechanism from that row and collide it with the fresh material.
   Mechanisms differ; phrasings of one mechanism do not count as variety.
3. Write the hook per the dynamic: text, sound, and first frame together,
   as ad-creative already requires.
4. Run the hub's gates unchanged: claims gate before writing, policy check
   (situation, never the viewer's attributes; urgency and scarcity only if
   true and dated), asset inspection at actual size.
5. Score glance on the ad as a whole once the turn lands, not on the hook
   alone. See the open ruling below.

## What this does not change

It does not replace ad-strategy: diagnosis, offer check, gap checks, plays,
batch rows, test design from the offer's own numbers. It does not replace
ad-creative's claims gate, policy check, or finish standard. It governs
only the opening's shape and the product's timing. Everything else in both
skills stands.

## Open rulings (not yet decided)

- Glance tension. The ad-creative rubric gives top glance marks when a
  stranger can say what is sold and for whom in one second. A curiosity
  hook deliberately withholds that in the first second, and the turn is
  what reveals it. Proposed ruling: glance is judged on the ad as a whole
  once the turn lands, not on the hook alone. For statics this is moot,
  since hook, turn, and close are simultaneous. For video it needs the
  ruling.
- Relevance by second two on video. The scene must signal who the ad is
  for early (object, setting, words, person) even while the product waits
  for the turn. This is compatible with the recipient-in-turn rule, but
  the two must be written together, not assumed.
- The Your Song row is untested. The framework's rows are drawn from
  collected craft material, not from this business's results. Learnings
  from this business's own results outrank it once they exist.
